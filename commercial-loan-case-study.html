Executive summary

This independent, simulated commercial real estate loan boarding case study demonstrates a controls-first approach to interpreting loan terms, calculating amounts due, reviewing escrow requirements, and documenting exceptions. All borrower details and numerical inputs below are invented for this publication; they are not copied from an employer assessment.

Outcome: an illustrative loan-boarding validation checklist, reproducible calculations, and recommendations for resolving exceptions before production boarding. This is a demonstration, not a completed bank transaction.

Illustrative loan scenario

Borrower: Harbor Point Residential LLC (fictional). Property: multifamily. Initial funded principal: $12,000,000. Annual fixed interest rate: 7.50%. Interest basis: Actual/360. Illustrative billing period: 31 days. Late charge: 5% of unpaid interest only, following a hypothetical grace period.

Illustrative outstanding principal at payoff: $11,700,000; prepayment charge: 2% of outstanding principal. Property value: $18,000,000. Annual tax escrow: $240,000; annual insurance escrow: $60,000. No reserve cushion is assumed. These terms are exercise assumptions, not statements about any real loan.

Methodology and control approach

1. Extract the loan agreement terms and funding details into a source-of-truth checklist. 2. Map fields to the boarding worksheet: borrower/CIF, beneficial owners, loan parameters, escrow, tracking, and funding. 3. Calculate each amount independently and cross-check the workbook formulas. 4. Flag missing documents, inconsistent terms, or unresolved exceptions. 5. Record source, calculation method, reviewer, and disposition before sign-off.

The source materials reviewed privately contained worksheets organized around CIF, beneficial ownership, loan parameters, escrow items, tracking, transactions/funding, codes, and formulas. This public exercise recreates that analytical structure without reproducing source records or identifying information.

Technical analysis: reproducible calculations

Interest (Actual/360): $12,000,000 × 7.50% × 31 / 360 = $77,500.00. Excel: =ROUNDUP(12000000*7.5%*31/360,2).

Illustrative late charge: $77,500 × 5% = $3,875.00. Excel: =ROUNDUP(77500*5%,2). Assumes only unpaid interest is chargeable and the grace period has elapsed.

Illustrative prepayment charge: $11,700,000 × 2% = $234,000.00. Excel: =ROUNDUP(11700000*2%,2). The actual charge would depend on the governing note, date, and exceptions.

Loan-to-value: $12,000,000 / $18,000,000 = 66.67%. Excel: =12000000/18000000, formatted as percentage.

Monthly tax escrow: $240,000 / 12 = $20,000.00. Monthly insurance escrow: $60,000 / 12 = $5,000.00. Combined monthly escrow = $25,000.00. Excel: =SUM(240000/12,60000/12).

Findings and exception review

Illustrative validation result: the calculated monthly interest, late charge, prepayment charge, LTV, and escrow splits reconcile to the scenario inputs. No actual bank records were boarded or approved as part of this simulated exercise.

Key exceptions to investigate in a real workflow: mismatch between the note and boarding rate; incorrect day-count convention; late fees assessed on escrow despite exclusions; missing beneficial ownership percentages; unsupported appraisal dates or values; and differences between closing funds and transaction entries. These are proposed control checks, not findings asserted about the original assessment.

Recommendations

Require a documented source reference for every material boarding field; use a second-person review for rate, maturity, interest basis, fees, and escrow settings; reconcile initial funding and settlement figures before posting; and maintain an exception log with owner, status, and evidence of resolution.

For a production environment, test edge cases such as partial periods, leap years, interest-rate changes, payoff timing, late-fee exclusions, escrow cushions, and amendment-driven term changes.
